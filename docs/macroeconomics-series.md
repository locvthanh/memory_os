# Macroeconomics Series — scene plan

A series of MemoryOS scenes for understanding macroeconomics. This is the
living design doc: edit it as scenes get built, and move each scene's
detailed notes into its own `claude/<scene>-scene.md` doc once work starts.

- **Status:** building — scene 0 (the Hydraulic Hall) is live
- **Started:** 2026-09-11
- **Scenes:** 1 hub + 9 concept scenes

---

## Design idea

Macroeconomics is mostly about **flows, stocks and feedback loops**: money
moving, piling up and pushing back. So the series is built around physical
things that flow (water, trains, a rollercoaster, a river of credit) rather
than monuments. MemoryOS is also an anti-library, so every scene ends on
something nobody knows yet.

### Rules shared by every scene

1. **Recurring cast.** The same five characters appear in every scene, so a
   face is enough to recall a role:
   - **The Baker** — a household *and* a firm (earns, spends, saves, hires)
   - **The Banker** — takes deposits, makes loans, creates money
   - **The Governor** — the central bank (holds the key to the dam)
   - **The Treasurer** — the government (taxes, spends, borrows)
   - **The Trader** — the rest of the world (imports, exports, currency)
2. **The Loaf of Bread.** A loaf with a price tag sits in every scene. Its
   price shows what money is worth there (normal, inflated, deflated,
   hyperinflated). It is also a callback to the bread monument in the WWII
   scene (besieged Leningrad).
3. **A real event at every stop.** Each concept is pinned to a real event
   (Weimar 1923, 1929, 1973, 1997, 2008, 2020, Đổi Mới 1986…).
4. **An open-question door at the end.** Each scene's last locus is a
   question economists still argue about: the anti-library door.
5. **Visually distinct.** Every scene gets its own art style (mixed styles
   across MemoryOS are deliberate). The cast and the loaf carry the
   continuity instead.

## 0. The Hydraulic Hall — the hub

- **Id:** `macro-hydraulic-hall` — **built 2026-09-11** (see *As built* below)
- **Concepts:** the circular flow of income; GDP = C + I + G + (X − M);
  leakages (saving, taxes, imports) and injections (investment, government
  spending, exports); the three ways to measure GDP (output, income,
  spending) all give the same number.
- **Real anchor:** the MONIAC, a water computer built in 1949 by the
  economist Bill Phillips to model the UK economy with water. (Phillips is
  the same man behind the Phillips curve in scene 3.)
- **Setting:** a tall hall around a giant glass-and-brass water machine.
  Tanks = households, firms, banks, government, rest of world. Pipes =
  wages, spending, taxes, imports, exports. Coloured water is money.
- **Art style:** steampunk — brass, copper, green glass, gaslight.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The Main Pump — spending drives everything (C)
  2. The Household Tank — the Baker's wages flow in, spending flows out
  3. The Firm Tank — output and income are the same water seen twice
  4. The Savings Drain and the Investment Pump (I)
  5. The Government Tank — tax valve out, spending pipe in (G)
  6. The Harbour Pipe — exports in, imports out (X − M)
  7. The GDP Gauge — three dials (output, income, spending), same reading
  8. Doors to scenes 1–9: each tank's valve is a portal (see Links)
  9. Open question: *Is GDP the right thing to measure at all?*
- **Motion needed:** water flowing through pipes; gauge needles moving.
- **As built (2026-09-11):** `blender_models/HydraulicHall.blend`, generated by
  `blender_models/hydraulic_hall_scenegen/hh_*.py`; live at
  `scene.html?id=macro-hydraulic-hall`. The machine is a flat, readable board on
  the north wall (a MONIAC blown up into the circular-flow diagram): households
  tank left, firms tank right, spending through the Main Pump along the bottom,
  income across the top, and the three side circuits nested between them —
  every leakage leaves households above, every injection enters firms below, so
  no pipe crosses another. Water colour = circuit (aqua money, green banks,
  amber government, blue rest of world); gold chevrons show flow direction
  (static — no animation yet). 11 loci: 1 the real 1949 MONIAC + Bill
  Phillips, 2 Main Pump (C), 3 households (Baker), 4 firms + the loaf,
  5 banks (S → I), 6 the Governor's interest-rate valve, 7 government
  (T → G), 8 rest of world (M → X), 9 the nine doors (west wall, not linked
  yet), 10 GDP gauge, 11 open question. Descriptions are real content.

## 1. The Vault of Money

- **Proposed id:** `macro-vault-of-money`
- **Concepts:** what money is (medium of exchange, unit of account, store
  of value); commodity vs fiat money; fractional-reserve banking; banks
  create money when they lend; bank runs; deposit insurance.
- **Setting:** a descent through rock strata; each layer is an era of
  money. The tour goes *down* in space and *forward* in time.
- **Art style:** geological cutaway — earth tones going into cold steel.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. Barter — the Baker swaps bread for shoes (and can't find a match)
  2. Cowrie shells
  3. The first coins — Lydia, ~600 BC
  4. Goldsmith receipts — 17th-century London; paper promises for gold
  5. Paper notes and the gold standard
  6. Greenbacks — Lincoln's paper money, 1862 (→ link to Civil War)
  7. Nixon closes the gold window, 1971 — fiat money begins
  8. The Banker's desk — a loan is made and new money appears out of
     nothing (the key idea of the scene)
  9. The Bank Run — a crowd at the doors (Northern Rock 2007, SVB 2023)
  10. Digital money and crypto
  11. Open question: *What makes money trustworthy — and could it fail?*

## 2. The Funhouse of Prices

- **Proposed id:** `macro-funhouse-of-prices`
- **Concepts:** inflation and how it's measured (CPI); demand-pull vs
  cost-push inflation; the wage–price spiral; inflation expectations;
  hyperinflation; deflation.
- **Setting:** a carnival funhouse. Distorting mirrors stretch the price
  tags; the loaf of bread grows or shrinks room by room.
- **Art style:** vintage carnival — striped canvas, bulbs, warped glass.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The CPI Basket — a shopping basket on a scale
  2. Demand-pull — a crowd crushing one stall (too much money, too few goods)
  3. Cost-push — an oil barrel squashing a factory (the 1973 oil shock)
  4. The Wage–Price Spiral — a spiral staircase that never ends
  5. The Fortune Teller — expectations: people expect prices to rise, so they do
  6. The Hyperinflation Room — Weimar Germany 1923 (a wheelbarrow of cash)
  7. Zimbabwe's 100-trillion-dollar note (2009)
  8. Vietnam 1986 — inflation over 700%, the road to Đổi Mới
  9. Deflation — Japan's frozen lake (the lost decades)
  10. Open question: *What is the "right" inflation rate — and why 2%?*

## 3. The Grand Station

- **Proposed id:** `macro-grand-station`
- **Concepts:** how unemployment is measured; frictional, structural and
  cyclical unemployment; the natural rate; the Phillips curve and why it
  broke in the 1970s (stagflation).
- **Setting:** a grand railway station. Workers are passengers; jobs are
  trains.
- **Art style:** art deco — marble, brass clocks, a vaulted glass roof.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The Departure Board — the unemployment rate
  2. Frictional — passengers waiting between trains
  3. Structural — the coal-mine platform whose line has closed
  4. Cyclical — every train cancelled by a storm (a recession)
  5. The Natural Rate — the station's normal crowd, never zero
  6. The Phillips Seesaw — inflation up, unemployment down (Phillips, 1958)
  7. The Broken Seesaw — 1970s stagflation: both up at once
  8. The Automation Hall — robots boarding the trains
  9. Open question: *Will AI create more jobs than it destroys?*

## 4. The Rollercoaster

- **Proposed id:** `macro-rollercoaster`
- **Concepts:** the business cycle (expansion, peak, recession, trough,
  recovery); aggregate demand and aggregate supply; leading indicators;
  the inverted yield curve; "animal spirits".
- **Setting:** a rollercoaster. The camera rides the track — the best fit
  for MemoryOS's on-rails tour in the whole series.
- **Art style:** a night-time amusement park, neon on dark.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The Climb — expansion
  2. The Peak — overheating
  3. The First Drop — 1929, the Great Depression
  4. The Oil Drop — 1973
  5. The Deepest Drop — 2008
  6. The Sudden Drop — 2020, COVID
  7. The Trough and the Recovery
  8. The Signposts — leading indicators; the yield-curve sign hangs upside-down
  9. Two Cranes — aggregate demand and aggregate supply
  10. The Carnival Crowd — animal spirits (confidence, fear)
  11. Open question: *Can recessions ever be abolished?*

## 5. The Dam

- **Proposed id:** `macro-dam`
- **Concepts:** what a central bank does; the policy interest rate; how a
  rate change spreads (the transmission mechanism); quantitative easing;
  the zero lower bound; policy lags; central-bank independence.
- **Setting:** the Governor runs a dam on a river of credit.
- **Art style:** brutalist concrete, with blueprint overlays.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The Governor's Control Room — independence (no politicians inside)
  2. The Sluice Gate — the policy interest rate
  3. Canal 1 — mortgages and house prices
  4. Canal 2 — business loans and investment
  5. Canal 3 — the currency (links toward the Port City)
  6. Canal 4 — stocks and bonds
  7. The Slow Water — policy works with "long and variable lags" (Friedman)
  8. The Reservoir Pump — quantitative easing after 2008
  9. The Open Gate — the zero lower bound: nothing left to lower
  10. Paul Volcker's Flood Gate — rates near 20% to kill 1970s inflation
  11. Open question: *Should central banks fight inequality or climate change?*

## 6. The Treasury Bathhouse

- **Proposed id:** `macro-treasury-bathhouse`
- **Concepts:** taxes and government spending; the deficit (a flow) vs the
  debt (a stock); the fiscal multiplier; automatic stabilizers; crowding
  out; government bonds; debt-to-GDP; sovereign default.
- **Setting:** a grand bathhouse. The deficit is the tap, the debt is the
  water level. The clearest possible picture of flow vs stock.
- **Art style:** Roman baths — marble, mosaics, steam.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. The Tax Drain and the Spending Tap
  2. The Bathtub — deficit vs debt
  3. The Ripple Pool — the multiplier
  4. The Shock Absorbers — automatic stabilizers (benefits rise, taxes fall
     in a recession without anyone voting)
  5. The Bond Auction — the Treasurer borrows from the Banker
  6. Crowding Out — government borrowing elbowing out private borrowers
  7. The Water-Level Gauge — debt-to-GDP
  8. Greece, 2010 — the bath overflows (default and bailout)
  9. Open question: *Can a country that prints its own money run out of it?*

## 7. The Port City

- **Proposed id:** `macro-port-city`
- **Concepts:** comparative advantage; tariffs and protectionism; the
  balance of payments (current account, capital account); exchange rates;
  fixed vs floating currencies; the impossible trinity; currency crises.
- **Setting:** a lantern-lit trading port, styled after Hội An.
- **Art style:** warm ochre walls, lanterns, junks and sailing ships.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. Two Islands — comparative advantage (Ricardo, 1817: English cloth,
     Portuguese wine)
  2. The Toll Gate — tariffs
  3. The Ship's Ledger — the balance of payments
  4. The Harbour Scale — exchange rates
  5. The Chain — a currency peg
  6. The Three-Key Vault — the impossible trinity: fixed rate, free capital,
     independent policy — you may turn only two keys
  7. The Typhoon — the 1997 Asian financial crisis (Thai baht, July 1997)
  8. The Container Crane — globalisation and supply chains
  9. Open question: *Is globalisation reversing?*

## 8. The Rice Terraces

- **Proposed id:** `macro-rice-terraces`
- **Concepts:** long-run growth; capital, labour and technology (the Solow
  model); diminishing returns; productivity; institutions; compounding.
- **Setting:** rice terraces climbing a mountain. Each terrace is one
  ingredient of growth; they widen as they rise, to show compounding.
- **Art style:** misty mountain terraces in soft greens.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. Labour — the farmers
  2. Capital — the tools (and why one more tool helps less each time)
  3. Technology — the magic seed (the only thing that grows forever)
  4. Institutions — fences and the rule of law
  5. The Rule of 70 — divide 70 by the growth rate to get the years to double
  6. The Industrial Revolution — the terrace where the steepness changes
  7. Korea vs Ghana — equally poor in 1960, worlds apart today
  8. Đổi Mới, 1986 — Vietnam opens up
  9. Open question: *What will AI do to productivity?*

## 9. The Glass Tower

- **Proposed id:** `macro-glass-tower`
- **Concepts:** asset bubbles; leverage; contagion; bank crises; the
  lender of last resort; the Minsky moment.
- **Setting:** a glass skyscraper whose floors are past bubbles; cracks
  spread upward as the tour climbs.
- **Art style:** glass and steel, cracking.
- **Loci (draft):**
  1. Tulip Mania — 1637
  2. The South Sea Bubble — 1720 (Isaac Newton lost a fortune)
  3. The Roaring Twenties and the 1929 crash (→ link to WWII: the road to war)
  4. The Jenga Stack — leverage
  5. The Dot-com Floor — 2000
  6. Subprime — 2008, Lehman Brothers
  7. Contagion — cracks spreading floor to floor
  8. The Safety Net — the lender of last resort (Bagehot, 1873)
  9. The Cliff — the Minsky moment: running off a cliff, then looking down
  10. Open question: *Where is the next bubble?*

## Links (how the series connects)

- **Into the world:** one of the empty "xx" gates on Luân Hồi Đài
  (portal-island loci 5, 6 or 8) becomes the door to the Hydraulic Hall.
- **Hub → scenes:** each tank or valve in the Hydraulic Hall is a portal to
  its concept scene; each concept scene's first locus links back to the hub.
- **Cross-links to existing scenes:**
  - Vault of Money → `civil-war` (greenbacks)
  - Glass Tower → `world-war-2` (the Depression and the road to war)
  - Funhouse of Prices → `world-war-2` (Weimar hyperinflation)
- Remember: the viewer supports one `link:` per locus.

## Technical notes

- **Animation playback is needed first.** The viewer can't play glTF
  animations yet (there's no `AnimationMixer` in `src/viewer/`). These scenes
  depend on motion: water in the pipes, the coaster, spinning gauges,
  growing price tags. Add animation playback to `Viewer.js` and export the
  animations in the glb (`export_animations=True`).
- **3D vs 2D.** All nine are planned as 3D. Candidates for the 2D
  (HTML/SVG) scene kind if a scene is really a chart: parts of the Port City
  (trade flows on a map, like Civil War).
- **Pipeline** as usual: Blender → `Locus_01…` Empties → `models/<id>.glb`
  → `src/scenes/<id>.js` → registry entry in `src/scenes/index.js`.
